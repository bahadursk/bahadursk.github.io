Teaching

Computation as a way of doing chemistry.

Since joining KL University in 2025, Dr. Bahadur Sk has designed three new courses that take chemistry students from computational chemistry through data science to machine learning for molecules, and coordinates Engineering Chemistry for about 2,000 B.Tech. students.

Courses Book Online book

Teaching philosophy

I teach computation as a way of doing chemistry, not as a separate programming subject.

Every concept starts from a chemical question, such as why a molecule is coloured or which structure will emit longest, and the code is kept as simple as possible so that students focus on the chemistry and the reasoning. Courses are built as a sequence: students first learn to think computationally and handle data, then work with molecular representations and machine learning, and finally apply quantum-chemical methods. I use hands-on notebooks every week, assess both understanding and application, and revise each course from outcome-attainment data.

Book

Molecules, Data and Python: Your First Steps in Chemistry Coding

Dr. Bahadur Sk · Notion Press · Paperback, 146 pages · ISBN 979-8909113266

How do chemists turn measurements into discoveries? Written for school students who have never coded before, the book teaches Python through the chemistry they already study: moles and concentration, pH, gas laws, titrations, reaction rates and periodic trends.

Across seventeen short chapters, readers write chemical calculations, clean laboratory data, draw honest graphs, fit calibration lines, simulate molecules on the move and train a first machine-learning model. Four mini-projects bring the skills together, and only a web browser is needed.

Open online book

Molecules, Data and Python: the interactive Jupyter Book

Dr. Bahadur Sk · Free to read online · Version 1.0 (2026) · Text CC BY 4.0, code MIT · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24583664

An open, interactive book that teaches Python through chemistry, from school level to research level, and applies machine learning across chemistry and materials science.

  • Part I Python fundamentals, every idea taught through a chemical problem
  • Part II NumPy, pandas, Matplotlib, Seaborn, Plotly, SciPy, SymPy and pint
  • Part III Cheminformatics with RDKit and 3D visualisation with py3Dmol
  • Part IV Kinetics, thermodynamics, spectroscopy, quantum chemistry, MD and crystals
  • Part V Machine-learning foundations for chemists
  • Part VI Machine learning across medicinal, organic, analytical and physical chemistry, catalysis, materials, energy, polymers, biochemistry, generative design and LLMs
