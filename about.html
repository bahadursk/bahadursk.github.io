Biography

My scientific curiosity began with a deceptively simple question: why do different materials emit, fade, or store light in different ways? That question has led my research from synthetic chemistry and spectroscopy into computation-guided design for modern optoelectronics.

At AURA Lab, I use DFT, TD-DFT and spin–orbit coupling calculations to predict how organic materials absorb, store and emit energy, computing the rate constants that decide whether an excited molecule fluoresces, phosphoresces or loses its energy as heat, and turning them into design rules. This work has led to strategies for selective triplet energy transfer, faster radiative decay and long-wavelength afterglow in purely organic materials. We are now building graph neural network models, trained on quantum-chemical data and interpreted with SHAP, to screen new organic optoelectronic materials at high throughput.

My teaching follows the same line. Since joining KL University I have designed three new courses that take chemistry students from computational chemistry through data science to machine learning for molecules, and I have written Molecules, Data and Python, a first course in chemistry coding for school students, together with a free interactive online edition that carries the same approach through to machine learning in chemistry and materials science.

Before joining KL University in August 2025, I was a Senior Research Associate at the DST Technical Research Centre, IACS Kolkata, a UEC Postdoctoral Fellow with Prof. Shuzo Hirata at the University of Electro-Communications in Tokyo, and a C. V. Raman Postdoctoral Fellow with Dr. P. Rajamalli at the Materials Research Centre, IISc Bengaluru. I completed my PhD in Chemistry (physical, materials and computational chemistry) at IISER Bhopal with Prof. Abhijit Patra, including a research internship at ENS Paris-Saclay, France.
